Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Answers adapted from the company's current service pages.

Is it better to repair my heating system or replace it?

Age and repair frequency are usually the biggest factors. If your system is more than 10 to 15 years old and needs frequent service calls, replacement often ends up being the more cost-effective choice. If your system is relatively young and the issue is minor, a repair is typically the smarter move. The same logic generally applies to air conditioning. Not sure? Call the team and talk it through.

How do I know if my furnace or boiler is the right size for my home?

Sizing matters. A unit that's too small struggles on the coldest days; one that's too large cycles on and off, wasting energy and heating unevenly. The right size depends on your home's square footage, insulation, and layout, which is why load is calculated for your specific home instead of using a generic formula.

What's the difference between a humidifier and a dehumidifier?

They solve opposite problems. A humidifier adds moisture to air that's too dry, typically a winter issue. A dehumidifier removes excess moisture from air that's too damp, more common in summer and basements. Some Philadelphia-area homes need both, at different times of year.

Can a mini-split keep up in a Philadelphia winter?

According to the company, modern mini-splits use heat pump technology designed to keep working as outdoor temperatures drop below freezing. Ask the team whether a system suits your specific space.

What does a UV light do, and do I need one?

UV lights install near the system's cooling coil, a damp environment where mold and bacteria can grow, and work quietly at the source. It's more about long-term air quality than immediate comfort. The team evaluates your home before recommending equipment.
